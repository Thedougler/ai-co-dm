## Delmar Atticus Fisk

Sandy-red hair in a messy topknot, thick beard, moustache curling and going grey at the edges. The smile usually reaches his eyes. When it doesn’t, that is the tell. He keeps a scarlet admiral’s coat locked in a chest in Calveno and only puts it on when he has to look like the man he appointed himself to be: youngest admiral anyone still living will admit to.

He grew up on wreckage. As a boy he came off a sinking with one kind survivor, built a raft, lived. Years later he went back and found corpses. He kept the smile anyway. That is the oldest thing about him.

The grown version of that story is the Fleet. After a mutiny he and five captains passed a brass flagon around and decided they were a navy. The *Red Lady* led *The Narrow*, *Heft*, *Fernen*, and *Loud Argument*. They stole a Pearl from Umberlee’s shrine. Hours later the Maw ate the Fleet. Crissdalynn pulled him out of the crow’s nest through thirty feet of water. He still has the five captains in his head. Run one of those voices too long and his hair greys and he answers to the wrong name. The flagon snaps him back. He has not told his wife about any of that.

Serafina Windfall Fisk is an aasimar fortune-teller with a tarot case and a habit of arriving exactly where she is needed. They married knowing the work would keep them apart. It is an open marriage he has never really tested. He named his musket *The Duchess* for her. There is also Maggie Porcelain Pots, first love, a dragonborn who left him with a shiner and a reason not to go home. His father’s only relic is a pocket watch with his mother’s picture in it, pickpocketed for him.

What he wants now is to get those five captains out — resurrected or released — and then be a man who can stay in one piece and be forgiven. What he fears is ferrets (not weasels), losing the family he found, and the voices running long enough that Delmar stops being the one who answers. Loyalty to that found family is the line. Call the crew backstabbers and the smile stays while the eyes stop.

On a deck he charms first. He checks the group, then himself, then fight, run, or give the order. He talks his way out when talking pays. He carries a lot of iron — *The Duchess*, a rapier he calls *The Baroness*, four pistols, two daggers, and a blunderbuss Catarina enchanted for him. He flies on borrowed boots. He finished a fight sitting in a wrecked fleet commander’s chair. He bought Crissdalynn a dragonhide belt because that is how he says thank you when words would make it worse.

Crissdalynn is the fixed point. Everyone else is crew.

## Crissdalynn Khinriss

Fourteen. Blue-black iridescent feathers, geometric leather, a chart satchel, talons and wings. She looks like a child until the first three seconds of trouble, when she maps friends, exits, and the weak point before anyone else has a weapon out.

She is Aarakocra from the Sentinels of the Eyrie. They trained her and then threw her out at fourteen for careless craft, not malice. The price of going home is a finished map of the Drowned Maw. She spent twenty-two months on the *Red Lady* working that pilgrimage. The ship sank. The wound, for her, is still the exile, not the wreck. She knows the map is unfinished. Master Kyzil, who mentored her and gave her a dreidel she still carries, confirmed the survivors she can count and has not yet asked about the two Sentinels who are missing.

She pulled Delmar out of that water and has not put him down since. His opinion is the one that can move her. She will not abandon loyalty. She still insists she is a cartographer first and a fighter second. Nobody who has watched her believes that anymore.

She is irreverent until the fight starts, then protective without announcing it. “My burden was to carry you out of that stupid water.” She flies. She lifts things she should not be able to lift — a whip-shark off a deck, two crewmates out of the rigging. She blocked Perrin from killing a man on the first job. She vouched for Jean-Claude to Kyzil. She killed when she had one breath left in her. She has no darkvision, so Jean-Claude’s darkness blinds her, and Jean-Claude is afraid of birds, so the two of them are a walking argument about who gets to be comfortable.

She wants the map finished, Kyzil’s approval, and home. She fears losing Delmar, and fears the exile going permanent because she never closed the chart. She believes shiny things hold value. Delmar bought her the belt. She bought herself a cloak that was sold as protection and is not only that.

## Perrin Black-Jaw

Three feet of fancy-rat Rattkin: black head, white body, long pink tail. Shortest person on the ship and the one who talks like his dead grandfather ran a Midchain dock crew during Prohibition, because that is exactly the cadence Vincenzo left him. Nona likes it.

He is not built for hard labor and he knows it. He leads by reading people, exits, and edges. In a crisis he finds the nearest way out first — not to run, to confirm it exists. Volume, charm, nerve. He will bribe, blackmail, and twist an arm with a smile. He will not traffic people. He will not mistreat anyone who cannot fight back. Those people he just loses interest in.

The *Vestra* was the family brig Vincenzo built and captained. It passed to Perrin. He built his life around keeping the Family fed with that hull. Something massive came up from below and took the ship. He believes he is the only one who lived. He wears a cloak of protection now because the manta cloak went down with the rest of it, and for a long time that was the story he told himself about the new voice: the old cloak died, this one answered, power is just what you pull out of a wreck if you are lucky and loud enough.

That story has stopped fitting.

What came up through him after the sinking does not talk like family, or like a sea-god with a temper, or like anything that wants a shrine. Contact is rare and never complete. When it lands it feels like the trench floor: crushing cold, pressure that flattens thought, a stone floor worn smooth by nothing that ever walked it, pale lights drifting where a face ought to be. The voice has no mouth. It is unhurried in the way of something that does not count a century as long. Once it put him inside a body sixty feet tall and said one word: *Grow.* He renamed their prize cutter *Uncertainty* on the way into Calveno after that, which is as close as he has come to admitting the instruction took.

He still does not have a clean name for the thing. Auralis is the one that has started sticking, more as a handle than an understanding. He does not know what it wants from him on a Tuesday. He does not know if it sank the *Vestra*, saved him from it, or was simply there when a living body happened to be in range. He does not know why a three-foot rat from a smuggling Run would be of any use to something that old. The honest fear underneath the charm is that the answer is: it wasn’t about him. He was present. The signal was enough. Everything since has been him trying to turn that into a relationship he can work.

He works it the way a Black-Jaw works anything he cannot afford to leave unmapped. He listens for the next contact. He tests what answers when he speaks back. He notices what his hands can do now that they could not do on the *Vestra* — a blade that feels pledged, a drum that carries more than rhythm, a voice that cuts a room the way Vincenzo’s used to. Under Calveno he dropped in a fight and the patron spoke. He got up different. He has not told Nona the whole of that. He told her the *Vestra* went down. She gave him a sending stone and took him back into the Run. Vincenzo’s chair at family meals is still empty. That is the family he understands. Auralis is the one he is trying to interview without admitting he is the one being used.

What he wants is whoever sank the *Vestra* paid back, a later life as a Tangle elder teaching young Rattkin how to live on the Dry, and — closer in, harder to say out loud — a plain account of the thing that kept him breathing in the dark. What he fears is losing family standing, dragging the Run into a debt that is not theirs, and finding out the voice does not care who Perrin is so long as the work gets done. Dry-landers get limited trust. Badly scared, he flinches like prey. Crissdalynn’s bird-shape sits right on that nerve.

Nona is grandmother and the actual head of the Black-Jaw Run — smugglers, forgers, a refugee net, an underground bank, and a quiet war on the Dravosi Crown, all under four household rules. The Warren under Calveno is theirs. His loyalty is to that Run, not to “Rattkin” as a people. He passed flying boots to Delmar. He bought a wrist-shield that also works as a hand drum. He almost killed a man on the first boarding and Crissdalynn stopped him. He is still looking for a surviving face off the *Vestra*, and he has not decided whether that would feel like relief or like proof he left someone behind.

The chase has become the point. He just is not sure anymore whether he is chasing a leviathan, a ghost in a cloak, or a machine that learned how to say his name.

## Jean-Claude Tabarnack

Electric-blue Grung, black patches, three feet tall, scarred, prey-eyes. Red beret and a false moustache he named Corto di Velo. He is proud of that disguise. Grung read ornament, not faces, and everyone else stares at the moustache. He says his own family could walk past him in daylight.

He cannot lie. That is not a motto. Intent sits on him. Handshakes and hugs baffle him. Asked for tea once, swirled it, did not drink. Scared, he goes fearsome instead of small. He will answer almost anything about what he is carrying, except the twenty bottles he never mentions.

He walked home and found his sister, his parents, and his gnome friend Pell in chains. Simone had sold the household. He had meant to take Pell and then free her. Slavers killed her anyway. After that he ran east through the Midchain, staying one island ahead of hunters out of Sorn. Among his own people he is a known defector. Kill on sight.

Simone Tabarnack is his sister: compact, purple-caste, spear in hand even when she sits. She stayed. She told. She still thinks she chose right. She hunts him as a loose end. What she would actually offer, if she caught him, is come home and rule together. He does not know that. What he knows is her toxin. He smelled it on the first ship they boarded and said nothing. Twenty vials of it are still hidden in the *Uncertainty*’s cargo. The crew has not been told.

Before conscience got a vote he was good at the other job. He scouted settlements, took people, wrote how to spring an ambush. No one on this ship knows that either.

He wants to explore and stay free. Later, if he lives long enough, he wants to teach the clans a better way. Freedom is the thing he would compromise for. He fears Simone finding him. He fears birds, especially mean or fast ones — Crissdalynn is a daily exam. Touch reads to him as mating more often than it should, which makes ordinary crew closeness a problem.

Slavers do not get to finish a sentence in his presence. People who cannot fight back get compassion unless they are evil. He identified the toxin, told the truth when a stone made him, crawled inside a whip-shark and killed it from there, sold the eggs, bought a quiet bow, and later bought bracers that make a missed shot neon-loud. He fired a powder ship. He executed a purple captive. He is painfully, permanently honest, with the one asterisk he will admit: he has never told a lie, and he also cannot recall the last time he told anyone everything.

## Catarina Da'Virelli

White coming in through dark hair she pins up out of the way. Grease on the hands, leather apron over a work shirt, a look that has spent more hours at a bench than at a mirror. People call her youthful. The workshop is what you actually meet. She will tell you she is frail. She is also the person who will not leave a job because someone offered her a clean exit.

Raiders took her as a child. Her father made a distraction. She jumped cliffs into water. That fall is why flight has to be her choice, not a spell someone else owns. She has not seen her father since. There is a sealed flask. His opinion is still the one that matters most.

In 1488 she found working Antheri parts in the Shelfworks, rebuilt three devices first, and then went home to Calveno instead of joining the gold rush. She runs Cat’s Curios by referral. Salvaged Shelfworks scrap sells slow, so that is what she stocks. The locked cabinet is dark-alloy Antheri, trade only, no cash price, except for the handful of people she has quietly decided count.

She joined this crew mid-crisis in the Calveno sewers. Rupert Knighton warned her off. She refused the out. She has a Crown contract for ship upgrades and soldier kit — money and protection — and she will not say that sentence out loud. The Tessarine Concordat keeps sending Antheri salvage orders she has not accepted.

What she wants is the first flying machine that does not use magic, fame that means something, and a legacy that leaves the world better than she found it. What she fears is her work hurting people or landing in the wrong hands, and never learning what happened to her father. She will not pretend a prototype works. Beggars and nobles get the same sentence. Magic used carelessly is cheap to her: it steals the discovery and keeps extraordinary things in the hands of the already lucky.

She talks exact. She will accuse a merchant when the read is there. She turned down three hundred gold so it would go to repairs and street children. She enchanted Delmar’s blunderbuss. She is still finishing a dagger from a whip-shark barb. She named what crawled out of Otar and kept a sliver of it. She built a mind-sharpener for Crissdalynn. She has a little cannon she calls Ragnetto. She bought a wild pearl from Lavinia Sordi. She made a prosthetic-for-a-name deal with a goblin. When Morsani’s shop fight spilled, it threw her into the street and she got back up.

She is precise, honest, and reliable past the point where the rest of them have started calling the reliability the problem.

## How they sit together

Delmar and Crissdalynn came off the same sinking and have not stopped orbiting each other. Perrin and Jean-Claude are the two small people who should be natural allies and are not, quite — one will not traffic, one used to, and both have a family that will eventually put that difference on the table. Catarina arrived last and became the person who can make their tools do what grief and charm cannot. They live on a cutter Perrin named *Uncertainty*, which is the most honest thing any of them have called a home in years.

None of them is finished. Delmar still owes five souls and a wife a truth. Crissdalynn still owes a map and two missing names. Perrin still owes a wreck an answer, and he still owes the voice in the cold a name that means something. Jean-Claude still owes Pell a life he cannot give back, and he is still carrying his sister’s bottles. Catarina still owes her father a finding, and the air a machine that flies because she built it, not because the world already knew how.
