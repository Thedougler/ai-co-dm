The Dravosi Crown is a colonial people who came west out of Tethyr and planted themselves in warmer water. Their charter still runs, in a thin line, through Port Nyanzaru and the last charted coasts of Faerûn; everything that matters to them now sits weeks of open ocean south of Chult, in the Verdant Scatter. They did not come as merchants first. They came as a court that needed harbours, a navy that needed timber and deep water, and a set of families who intended to keep a crown on a map that did not already belong to them.

On paper they are one polity. In the islands they are a transplanted homeland. Human-majority, garrisoned, stamped, and sure of the flag. They call the western islands the Crown Islands because that is what they made of them: Calven, Harwick, Aldenmere, Stenmark, with Calder’s Tooth sitting like a gatepost at the mouth of the Central Strait. Pale stone forts sit on the headlands. Cannon cover the approaches. The Dravosi colours fly at every major anchorage. Between the islands the Greyteeth reefs break the surface in long pale ridges and have wrecked three generations of charts. Local pilots charge what they are worth. Patrols keep the big harbours honest. They do not keep the whole sea.

That is the first thing to understand about them. The Crown does not claim to love the water. It claims to rate it.

**How they live**

Harwick is the army place. The old word is still in the name — *hær-wīc* — and the island has never pretended to be anything else. The Naval Yard there has built every class of Crown warship for six generations. The Admiralty sits behind the same walls. The Bight on the southwest shore is the largest anchorage in the Scatter that will take a ship of the line, and the Crown fortifies that fact the way other people fortify a city. Civilians in Harwick Town sell rope, lodgings, tar, and drink to the rating pool. Nothing past the Yard gate opens without Admiralty say-so. No Harwick sailor swims off the wharf. Hunter sharks work the scraps from the slipways.

Calven is older, lower, and wetter. Marsh and tidal flats, farms on the middle plateau, and one city on the only solid ground that would take a foundation: Calveno, on the southeast coast. The Dravosi flag flies at the harbour. The money does not. Tessarine houses hold six of the seven Council seats. The city talks Italian in the canals — Alvise, Lavinia, Grimani, Sordi — because the Concordat wrote the ledgers first. Under Le Paludi the Rattkin have been in the old drains longer than either colonial power, and they prefer the official story to stay that way.

Aldenmere is the quiet island. Farms, hedges, a tidal mere a mile across that shallows in summer and deepens in spring. The Crown claims it, sends a revenue officer twice a year for the fishing tithe, and leaves. Stenmark is stone cliffs and cold deep water, the easternmost of the chain, with a signal station watching the discoloured water where the Drowned Maw begins. High Eyrie sits beyond that, a basalt stack the Sentinels have held for two hundred years. The Crown has offered them three deals. They have refused all three.

Tidefall is not one of the big islands. It is the checkpoint. Three tiers on the cliff of Calder’s Tooth: docks at the water, a trade shelf on the face, Fort Crestwall on the crown with the guns. Every ship entering the western Strait is supposed to stop at the Inspection Pier, answer questions, and show papers. Most do. Charts are sold over a counter. Vessel ratings are written down. Black nooses hang over the gate and no one can tell you when they went up. Governor Voss holds the civic seat and is kept out of the Admiralty’s sealed orders, which is a very Dravosi arrangement: two authorities in one harbour, and the one with the guns does not always copy the one with the petitions.

**What they believe**

They swear by Tyr. Not the sea’s Tyr — there isn’t one — but the court’s. Oaths, commissions, courts martial, the warrant that names a guilty person. Garrison chapels smell like paper that has been sworn on. Scales cut into a lintel, a warhammer where another faith would hang a lantern. Sailors still pay Umberlee at the dockside shrine because the wave does not care who stamped your commission. Tyr takes the fort and the bench.

That split is ordinary to them. A Dravosi officer can tithe the Bitch Queen in the morning and sit a rating board in the afternoon without feeling like a hypocrite. The sea is a fact. The document is a claim. They live in the gap.

They talk like clerks even when they are armed. Stamps, rank, vessel class, heresy confirmed or not confirmed. When the paper is thin they stall. When heresy is confirmed they stop stalling. They do not send speeches ahead of a boarding. They send an inspector, then a pair of enforcers in heavier grey coats with a cutlass and a hooked pole, then — if the matter is ordinary crime — a court. They do not send Aleksander Malone for ordinary crime.

Malone is the part of the faith they keep in a box. High elf, Tyr’s cleric, held at Blackrule until someone with rank says the word heresy. They tried giving him a handler once. The handler did not survive. The standing proverb in the service is that their dog needs a long leash, and a handler will only get bit. They loosed him in public at Sarn’s Landing and the results were ugly enough that the reservation exists now as policy, not preference. He still believes the violence was judgment. The Crown believes it was a paperwork problem they do not intend to repeat in a harbour people can see.

**How power actually works**

The public Crown is inspection law. Letters of marque. Garrison posts. A sitting monarch whose name is not spoken in the ordinary files — the succession is dynastic, three centuries of noble families from one crowning, and the offices run whether or not anyone on a cutter can tell you who wears the thing.

The private Crown is older than the first Dravosi ship that reached the Scatter. Aldric Drave has been a lich since before that landfall. He built the colonial project as a bloodline map. Every noble house the Crown raised carries some of him. The ledgers, the lineage books, the deep archive files that a factor like Aldous Draves can spend thirty years knowing how to read — those are Aldric’s chart of the living. He keeps sealed vaults in the cliffs under Fort Crestwall. Two garrison detachments hold the fort on orders the Governor never saw. Destroy the body and he comes back through the descendants. The public service does not know this. Barnaby Rook served the bureaucracy Aldric built and never learned the founder’s name. That is the point of a good archive. It outlives the people who think they run it.

Blood thins. It always does. Aldric’s long work is to pick the strongest sorcerer left in the line, inhabit that heir, and start the three-century cycle again. He favours the Draves branch. Geoffrey Draves is a nineteen-year-old carpenter who put a sword down rather than die underpaid on a privateer’s cutter; Aldous is the Tidefall import factor who placed him there. House Knighton sits close enough to that same web that a commodore and a clerk can share a surname-pattern without sharing a dinner table.

**The fleet**

They prefix their hulls HCS. Harwick builds them. Grey hulls, white Admiralty stripes, brass, pennants, figureheads that hold a crown up like a claim rather than a gift.

The *Sovereign* is the peak of that claim. First-rate, more than a hundred guns, copper-sheathed, six years on the stocks. She does not patrol. She does not escort. She arrives when the Crown needs the whole Scatter to see the flag at once. Word of her coming runs weeks ahead. She presents guns and waits. She cannot turn in the shallows. She is not sent after a stolen cutter.

The *Ordinance* is the other big hull people actually meet: three decks, Tyr blind in gold scales on the bow, a court that floats. She ends pirate gangs by being seen in a lane. Corbin Knighton has her now — young, scarred, Tessarine-trained, the new one in charge of the knights. Rupert Knighton is the commodore behind him, last-resort fleet, a man who does not shout. Officers who have watched him work say he does not threaten. He replaces. He calls the vessels under him Knights instead of names. Captain Gorgeous was close enough to that house to matter. Barnaby Rook was a tool on the same board, a hard privateer working the Tidefall shakedown with papers in his cabin that looked official and read vague.

The workaday Crown is cutters, inspection boats, and the pair of men who come across your rail. Deckhands first. Enforcers if you argue. An alchemist in the complement if the cargo smells like a problem. They know Midchain shipping lanes, inspection procedure, and how long a distress signal takes to reach a fort. That last number is why pirates still work the Greyteeth. The Crown is strong where the forts can see. It is a rumour where they cannot.

**Where the grip holds, and where it does not**

West of the Galewall, and in the Crown Islands themselves, they can enforce. Tidefall stops you. Harwick can bury you in hulls. Calveno will fly their flag while Tessarine houses count the debt. East of the Galewall the map thins. By the Midchain they have lost the ability to do much more than run cutters down the main lanes. Smart captains take the back channels and the Doldrums. Kalowe dry-docks anyone who pays and asks nothing about cargo. The Passage runs entirely outside colonial record. Rattkin are in every Crown port; the official relationship with the Passage is hostile, and the Warren likes it that way.

The Tessarine Concordat is the other charter in the same sea: Amnian merchant-house model, credit instead of courts martial, Calveno instead of Harwick. They are rivals who share harbours. The Crown inspects. The Concordat invoices. Both collect from the same fishermen. Both pay Umberlee because the tribute system is infrastructure, not a sect. Neither has solved the Grung interiors of the Verdant Teeth, so both buy what they can at the beach. The Crown’s toxin pipeline runs through Simone Tabarnack — substitute stock, not Karath farm product — and that flask has already turned up in privateer kit and in Calveno’s sewers. Institutional cruelty sits behind the document. Lavinia Sordi left a Crown prison records desk over it, smuggled confiscated goods on the way out, and spent nine clean years with a canal commission before she would sell a curse in the open. She will not name the facility. She does not have to. Anyone who has watched a rating form decide a life already knows the tone.

There are arrangements the Crown will not write down. An adult blue dragon works the northern Greyteeth. Captains who fly the right flags and keep an open chest on deck survive runs that the ledgers file as wrecks. Harbourmasters know which stamp a cargo needs. Shore control does not make the reefs safer. Signal logs at Stenmark hide patterns Harwick has not noticed. Extra companies have arrived at Crestwall under sealed orders, with reef-diving gear and underwater charges marked for the Drowned Maw, and the Governor still has not been told why.

**What they look like from the deck**

Grey coats. Correct dress uniforms, some of them cut to a pattern two hundred years out of date if you know how to see it. White cravats on the men who give orders once. Salt and gun oil. A harbourmaster’s boat alongside before your anchor is down. Gulls, tar, stone. Names that sound like Tethyr and the western homelands — Drave, Draves, Knighton, Voss, Fenn, Rook, Malone — sitting next to Venetian canal names they never quite absorbed. They rate your hull. They commission your privateer. They court-martial your officer. They will hunt heresy with a man they cannot leash and call that justice because Tyr’s hammer is on the lintel.

They are not a mystery cult. They are a colonial service that learned, over three centuries, that a seal will travel farther than a speech and last longer than the official who inked it. The kindness in that system is whoever happens to be holding the stamp. The cruelty is the same object. East of their forts the paper still looks official. The sea does not have to honour it, and increasingly it doesn’t.
