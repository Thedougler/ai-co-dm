The Grung are a small, wet-skinned people who live in the rainforest islands they call the Verdant Teeth. Water is not scenery to them. It is how they stay alive. They breathe air and water both, and if they go a full day without soaking for about an hour they start to fail in a way only another soak will fix. Their skin is vivid and always damp. Touch it and you take their poison. They can jump a distance that looks wrong on a body that size, climb wet wood like it was a stair, and pull with a tongue if they need a hand they do not have. Color is the first thing you notice, because color is how their world is ordered.

They do not keep towns a chart would mark. They live in canopy, standing pools, flooded cuts, and the mid-story of forest so old the floor is just fallen trees and roots. A path that lasts was left on purpose. From open water the Teeth look like one green wall with a pale reef ring. Jungle comes down to the tide. There is no harbour wall and no town smoke. Their boats work the gaps and do not invite a second look. Beach trade only. Interior closed. A hull that noses a reef gap gets a dart, not a hail.

Five islands do the work.

**Veth** is the big one: high dry-ground rainforest, limestone rising straight off the reef, trees tall enough to make their own weather. One sanctioned western beach, twenty strides of sand, no pier. Blue go-betweens come down a living root-stair, trade bone, resin, and pigment for food, timber, metal, and rope, and send you back to the water. Higher caste watches from the treeline and does not come down for a hull. Nobody on Veth speaks to a gold in front of outsiders.

**Sorn** is the farm island. Seasonal rivers lay silt, the Botukuri clan works the flood lanes, and the forest takes back whatever they cannot hold. One guarded pale beach. Purple soldiers hold the sand. Traders still wait offshore and stop at the waterline. Raids do not launch from here. This is also where Jean-Claude Tabarnack came from, Botukuri and blue, and where his sister Simone still keeps the garrison.

**Karath** is the loud island. Blackwater rivers the color of old tea, air that bites the throat, no sanctioned landing. This is where the Teeth’s poison race is winning. Gold keeps hidden toxin farms in the interior that even lower caste are not told about. Behind long huts set back from the beach, captives work wet pens that grow ordinary vermin into things the size of nightmares. Fighting-age men taken off Midchain coasts go into that pipeline as slave-soldiers. Midchain villages already file the raid complaints and have no way past the gaps.

**Dreth** has no shoreline you can name. The forest stands in the tide. Canopy joins overhead. Timber crews work the rare dry hummocks. The channel between Dreth and Orak is flooded jungle, axolotl water, and forbidden ground.

**Orak** is the stone jaw: karst towers in cloud, forest jammed into clefts, white water falling off the face into Dreth. No trade beach. Shafts that breathe cold. The clans count the holes and do not explain them.

They are clans, plural, not one tidy kingdom. Botukuri is the farm name Midchain ledgers have used for thirty years. Other clan names stay inside the canopy. On Sanvashti, south in the Midchain, rival clans meet on old mossed terraces under a truce older than anyone at the fires. Different castes sit at different fires and watch each other more than they watch a visitor. Nobody draws a weapon. That is the whole point of the place.

What binds the clans is caste, and caste is color.

Gold leads. Red keeps memory and the toxins. Purple fights. Blue handles scouts, intermediaries, and slaves. Green labors. Lower caste reports. It does not hold a line unless someone of handler rank is watching. Green sentries hide, let traffic pass, flee, and tell. Purple holds the beach and the raid. Blue talks to outsiders and moves people. Red keeps the recipes and the stories. Gold does not negotiate.

Everyone is taught that color is destiny written in the body. The true sequence is plainer and uglier. Color starts in the diet. What you eat and what you soak in stains the skin. Gold then finishes the job with a sealing rite they do not admit exists. The rare toxin that makes true gold-tier color grows only on those hidden Karath farms. Without the farm toxin you can stain a body. You cannot finish a gold.

That is the first layer of what outsiders call their magic, and it is not magic at all. The poison in the skin, the leap, the tongue, the way diet dyes them — that is just how a Grung body works. A person who can see spells will see nothing on any of it.

The second layer is the stolen work. Gold built a rite called Ossketh. It is learned technique, not prayer: a transmutation meant to be cast on someone else, completed by a second pair of hands, and then locked. Done that way, the new color holds. No daily upkeep. Cast it on yourself and it never finishes. The change stays open. It needs another mage to keep patching it, or it starts to slip. Ozzeth found the rite first. His skin ran blue and red at once, unreadable, and Gold branded him Twiceborn — not a title he chose, a censure they used so they would not have to admit the rite was real. He kept Simone’s version stable until he died covering her work in Calveno. Simone had cast it on herself. She wanted gold. Crown payment was a substitute toxin, not the Karath farm stuff, so she stalled at partial gold: purple-and-black skin broken into dots and bands of gold she never hides. She still believes diet plus the rite is the whole machine. She has never seen a real seal finish. Gold tolerated her and never legitimized her. They punished with reputation, not soldiers, because a public trial would have confirmed the trick.

The third layer is the one that will matter later. Lower caste does not think Gold are clever priests. They think Gold are living gods. That belief is a tap. When the congregation is close and sure, power runs through a gold the way a current runs through a wet wire — not from them, through them. At full tap they can do work that looks like weather, command, and decree. Take them off the Teeth, break the faith in the room, or starve the crowd, and the tap thins in hours. Lower caste does not know this. The thing at the top of that stack is the Gold Caste Serene: a small amphibious body you cannot look at directly, hovering over the most sacred ground in the Teeth, attended by hundreds. It does not fight and it does not bargain. It issues decrees. The jungle answers. Outside the Teeth the tap starves.

So the culture is not “frog people with poison.” It is a closed island people who turned biology into law, then turned law into a church, then hid the workshop that makes the church possible. They are losing on the water and they know it. Their ships cannot project far from shore. Poison is the equalizer. Every raid is a calculation they can barely afford. They sell toxin to the Dravosi Crown even while they raid Crown and Concordat coasts. They press-gang fighting-age men out of festival ports — Calveno first, Midchain next — and feed them into Karath. When they are cornered they poison wells and spend civilians. Ussa, a low-caste scout who defected, takes bribes on the cut-line and spends them buying kin out of slavery. That is the other face of the same order: people inside it who already know the story is a cage.

What they want, stated out loud, is the Teeth, the beaches, and another year. What the golds are building toward is a closed theocracy that exports poison on its own terms and never has to explain the farms, the rite, or the tap. If nobody breaks the congregation, they become a static, ugly, hard country that the rest of the sea learns to sail around.
