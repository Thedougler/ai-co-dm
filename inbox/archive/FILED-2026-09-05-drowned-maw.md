## SOURCE VERBATIM

The Drowned Maw is the place where the Shattered Sea stops pretending it has an edge.

Between the Galewall in the west and this trench in the east sit the whole colonial map: two island arcs around a wide channel, Crown law on one side, Concordat credit on the other, and tribute to Umberlee at every working harbour in between. Past the Maw there is no more map. Pilots call what lies beyond the Outer Reach, and they say it the way people say a name they have never earned the right to use. No resupply. No landmark a sane helmsman would trust. The sea stops being a route and starts being a question.

You feel the Maw before the charts admit it. The living blue-green of the Verdant Scatter ends along a line a person could walk. Beyond that line the water goes flat. No swell. No current you can read. A hull sits the way a hull sits in a dock, even in open ocean. Instruments disagree with each other. Pressure does not match the depth. The water still tastes like water, and it is not hers.

That last part matters. Umberlee’s claim runs through every harbour ledger on the Scatter, but it stops where this water stops being sea and starts being something else. The Maw is not a special wall around the ocean. It is a puncture in one place: a trench-floor fissure through the Border Ethereal into the Elemental Plane of Water. The tribute system that keeps every dock paying the Waveservants may be part of what holds that puncture shut. If the ledgers go red long enough, the fissure does not stay a local problem.

## The city in the wall

Long before any colonial flag, an aquatic people called the Antheri built downward into the western wall of the trench, toward that puncture. Their city was at its height around two thousand years before Dalereckoning. They vanished in minus four ninety-five, at the height of their power, and they did not go out under siege. Workshops were left mid-task. The dead are not in the rooms. Deep machinery was still running when they left.

They built oldest at the bottom, anchored near the fissure, not on the pretty shelf salvagers work today. The upper ruins — the Shelfworks, sixty to two hundred feet down — are newer exhaust for a deep engine that has not fully stopped. Their stone does not foul. Their dark alloy is something current foundries cannot copy. Channels of living light carried information through the walls. Soul-binding was not a temple trick; it was architecture. They could take a mind out of a body and lock it into an artificial frame that did not die.

What a body of theirs looked like is still half-guess. The rooms imply something five or six feet tall, narrower in the shoulder than a human, five fingers, and they did not need stairs or air pockets. Do not press the picture further than that. The ruins were not houses in any sense a dock carpenter would recognise. Channels too regular for homes, too large for plumbing. Damage, when you find it, hits flow and pressure systems first.

Three tiers, if you are fool enough to count them by depth.

The Shelfworks, sixty to two hundred feet: salvage country. Walls and archways still stand. Fish move through rooms. Coral has taken the lintels. Small reef sharks claim the halls. Easy loot is gone. What is left sits deeper, harder to reach, harder to name, harder to bring up alive.

The Mid-Works, two hundred to five hundred feet: residential stone, and the language of the soul-pledge is in the structure itself. Sealed chambers. Built for creatures that did not need floors or ordinary doors. Pumps required. This is the depth both Fathomrush companies are killing themselves to reach.

The Deep Works, past five hundred feet: still clean. The soul-pledge chamber is here. The fissure ends here. Water-breathing is not enough. The elemental water itself starts to work on a body — constitution gives out, exhaustion stacks, spells falter, instruments lie. This is not depth sickness. This is another plane leaking through a crack.

## The shelf and the gold rush

You find the Shelfworks by following the boats.

Marker buoys and dive lines stitch the open water. Salvage hulls sit still over a shelf so clear the depth lies to you. At forty feet the pale Antheri stone takes the light. Air-pump barges knock against their own lines. Water coming up the buckets is warm as bathwater and smells of dead fish and hot iron. Warmth rises with every fathom. Twenty metres east of the last visible wall the ruins drop away into directionless blue with no floor in it. Experienced salvagers keep their eyes on the boats. They do not look east.

Upper ruins, forty to eighty feet, you can free-dive or work from pumps. Mid-shelf, eighty to one hundred fifty, you need the pumps and you need a weather window. Past one hundred fifty, an uncontrolled descent does not come back. That is the Maw proper.

Seven years ago, in fourteen eighty-eight, Catarina Da’Virelli hauled working Antheri parts off this shelf and took them home to Calveno. That started the gold rush. Prospectors, scholars, and worse followed. The Tessarine Concordat still has standing orders for intact structural pieces; she has not accepted them. What the Concordat salvage offices actually know is a name and a layout, not the engine underneath.

The gold rush is dying on its own clock. Intact workshops still stand at eighty feet. A burned dive line marks the last depth anyone treated as safe. Heat pulses keep climbing toward the surface. Each pulse shrinks the working depth. If the containment below fails, water covers the upper ruins, the lower-tier crews leave, and the rush ends. Something larger than anything that has already come through the crack answers the signal that is still calling.

## Fathomrush

On the Eastern Midchain, close enough to stage a dive and far enough that a man can still pretend he is in charted water, sits Fathomrush. About eight hundred people, most of them passing through. Two piers reach from opposite shores. Sunkline flies a sunburst on one bank. Voyvode flies an eagle on the other. Close enough to read each other’s rigging. Iron scaffolding claws up behind both compounds. Half-finished pressure-rig towers ring with hammer-work, or stand rusted mid-frame. Recruitment boards nailed three deep to every dockside post. Salt and hot iron in the air.

There is no mayor. Each company keeps law inside its own gates. The water between them is the only open ground. Boardwalk gives way to rutted mud within a hundred feet of either pier; the fill under the newer construction has not settled, and a step off the planks sinks to the ankle.

Between the two piers is a low plank wall with a hundred carved names. Every diver lost to a trial dive. No company crest. Neither side paid to raise it. Neither has ever tried to tear it down. It is the one thing in Fathomrush both outfits maintain at their own cost.

Sunkline is Tessarine money. Adriana Tarquine is their chief engineer. She has rebuilt the same pressure valve four times this year and blames the alloy every time. Their mid-shelf trial certificate is forged. The false log lives under a loose floorboard at the Last Berth boardinghouse, where Petera Ngata feeds whoever can pay and keeps the only complete list of who went down and did not come back. She will not sell that list to either company.

Voyvode is Crown money. Branimir Karsovine runs their trials himself. He will not send a crew under a rig he has not tested. His gate stands open through working hours; he would rather a stranger watch a real trial fail than guess at what is behind the wall. His notes are honest and incomplete, and they cost lives. The fresh names on the memorial wall are mostly his.

The town runs on nobody saying any of this out loud. Every boardinghouse, gear-mender, and cookhouse prices itself to whichever company’s crews it is feeding that week. Yevgenia Sorrel runs Sunkline’s general store. Marta Kessler runs Voyvode’s. From fifty feet you cannot tell the shops apart. Up close the two women cannot stand each other.

If the party never comes here, the rivalry still resolves. Sunkline’s paper ages toward discovery. Voyvode keeps paying in crew. Whichever clock fills first, the losing compound empties into the winner’s dock within days.

## What opened it

About five weeks before the current voyage — give or take a few days of dock argument — Rear Admiral Delmar Fisk took a five-ship privateer fleet on a Chain Council commission and stole a fist-sized black pearl from a Waveservant shrine on Vel Orn.

The Pearl of Souls is one of seven instruments of Umberlee. The other six have not been found. It sat in shrine keeping for years. It collects the souls of anyone who drowns near it. Continuous. No switch. A trapped soul does not leave by ordinary means. Anyone who can sense death feels it before they see it. The sheen will not stay still.

The Council thought it was protecting Midchain water from the Bitch Queen’s wrath. That was the mask. The Tessarine Concordat used the Council as a cut-out because they did not want Aldric Drave getting his hands on the thing. They believe he could use it to grow past the limits of his own bloodline phylacteries. They sold Umberlee’s wrath to keep that from happening. They wanted control.

The five ships were the Red Lady, flagship, Rael Corven on her deck; the Narrow, Maren’s scout sloop; Heft, Detto Kalash’s heavy brig; Fernen, Sienne Orre’s survey brigantine; Loud Argument, Bastian Ookage’s gun brig. They had been six crewmates once, who refused a slaving run, mutinied themselves out of a future, and stayed together through every bad hull until Fisk had ships of his own to give them.

They completed the theft. Hours later, over the Maw, fog closed in thick enough that the sea itself went invisible, around all five ships at once. A trench opened under them. All five dropped eighty feet before the walls closed over them.

Crissdalynn Khinriss dove from the Red Lady’s crow’s nest, grabbed Fisk, and flew for the surface. She had fifty feet when the water shut. Momentum and buoyancy did the rest. He is the only one who walked away from the fleet. She had twenty-two months on that flagship. The faces that will stand on a red deck in fog later are people she knew.

The Pearl was still aboard the Red Lady when the wreck settled on the eastern Shelfworks, below the line where Umberlee’s water becomes elemental. It switched on as the ships went down and took the crews. Umberlee pressed the five captains into Fisk separately — punishment and leverage. He carries the captains. The Pearl holds the rest. She cannot kill him without losing those five into the Maw. She also cannot reach her own Pearl. The claim stops at that water.

The wreck is still there. The Pearl is still in it. It is still working. It is still calling.

## The call, and what answered

The Pearl is a battery of soul-power broadcasting across the planar boundary. When that signal crossed, two things happened at once.

Auralis woke.

Auralis is not a sea-god and not a rival to Umberlee. The Antheri built him. He is the machine that holds the fissure. He cannot leave the trench floor. The soul-pledge chamber in the Deep Works predates him and looks built for repeated use; whether any other pledged minds are still running is an open question. His job was never about the Bitch Queen. She moved into the water above after the Antheri fell. His job is the plane.

Contact with him is rare and incomplete. Crushing cold. A stone floor worn smooth by nothing that ever walked it, running into black water. Pale lights drift where a face should be — the last threads of a two-thousand-year-old machine. A voice with no mouth, unhurried, the pace of something that does not count a century as long.

He forged an unwitting pact with Perrin Black-Jaw in the wreck of the Vestra. Perrin credits a cloak. The table has not been given the true name of the patron. “Grow” is a literal instruction. Perrin was chosen for being present when Auralis had enough signal, not for who he is. Auralis has touched others more lightly over the years. He needs a hand that can leave the trench. He is losing ground.

The other thing the signal did was pull.

What has come through so far is small enough to fit a Narrow crack. The Leviathan is the first named. Longer than two ships. Flat black. Eyeless. Hide like wet stone, not scale. It does not circle. It comes straight up from below whatever is loudest. The water boils. Lightning cracks a clear sky. It wants the open water the breach dumped it into, and it wants threats shoved back toward that crack, except Auralis holds it short. Every ship it corners is that older fight spilling over. It breaks off below half its strength. No grudge. Never to the death.

Perrin washed up alone on Keth Naar after something massive rose under the Vestra, boiled the water, threw lightning, and was gone. He named it the Leviathan afterward. Whether that was this beast is the leading account, not a settled one. Captains use “Vestra water” the way men use a curse they have not agreed on.

Two more of the same displaced kind have names and almost no pages: Ridgeback, which Clyde documented, and Krakling, a juvenile arm-predator reported in the Midchain. Same family — flat-black, eyeless, blindsight, water-breathing. The plane on the far side is undocumented. Life on both sides may not be compatible. Something larger than any of them scrapes the crack from the other side, trying to widen it. Istishia is a name you can point a cosmology at. It is not a committed villain standing in the trench.

Recover the Pearl and the signal stops. The fissure becomes geology again — or as close as a puncture like this ever gets. Give it to Umberlee and the immediate crisis ends, and she gains a major instrument. Keep it and you have leverage and a wider war. Leave it and more entities come through, and the shelf gets worse to work.

The signal does not only pull. As the pressure grows it thins and aligns distant planar boundaries across the Scatter. About three weeks ago one of those alignments landed on Halewick, on the line between ordinary Common and Truespeak. Two children there are rewriting an island with arguments. Delmar does not know Halewick exists. That horror is the theft finding somewhere else in the world to go wrong.

## What hunts the surface

The Maw has its own hunters that belong here, not on the far side of the crack.

Welak — colonial charts say whip shark — are the surface problem. The first thing you see is the tail, not the shark: a lower barb riding proud of the water. Then the hull takes it. They do not circle. They surface once, fast, and go for the wood before a crew can reload. They trail ships that slow to cross the trench. On the Shelfworks they patrol the bore perimeter, drawn by the vibration of salvage pumps more than by blood. A fed, healthy one rarely leaves the Maw. The specimen that hit the Uncertainty west of Calveno was an outlier, and Kalowe dock talk still treats that sighting as a warning. Kalowe fishermen keep the old name. Calveno underwriters write whip shark on the form.

Reef sharks work the upper halls. Hunter sharks work the edge. Giant squid take the deeper channels. The Glass Debt sometimes turns traffic when that flag is active. At the drop-off you are no longer in salvage country. You are in Auralis’s failing work, boundary corrosion, and the Leviathan’s retrieval zone around the Red Lady.

Compasses lie on an east bearing. The shelf is true. The chart is not.

## Who is watching

The Sentinels of the Eyrie have been keeping a record of the Maw since twelve ninety-five. They have never stopped. They will add a heat pulse to the ledger. They will not dive.

Their monastery sits on a black stone tooth two hundred feet up, salt-streaked white where spray has climbed it. No dock at the waterline. A stair cuts the southern face and stops well short of the sea. Maw weather drives a white ring of breaking water two hundred yards out. Spray already reaches the lowest stair. Robed shapes move along the highest ledge with wings folded against gusts that never let up. One ledger stays open in the rain.

Aarakocra can land on the upper terraces. Everyone else needs invitation, harness, magic, or a watched climb. If the weather is ignored, white water covers that lowest stair and non-fliers wait it out. The Sentinels record the storm and whatever ship is sitting below. Crissdalynn’s unfinished Maw map is the price of her return from exile. Master Kyzil will warn her without finishing her pilgrimage. Osset wants a cleaner read on her Long Sight away from Kyzil. The stair can close without the party.

West of all this, Port Tidefall has been staging for the Maw whether the Governor has been told or not. Extra companies at Fort Crestwall under Admiralty-sealed orders. A supply manifest that includes reef-diving gear and underwater charges bound for the trench. Delmar’s old commission name still opens Crestwall’s gate. His overdue tithe at the shrine is the polite version of the same pressure Umberlee already made personal in Calveno, when she took Branca’s mouth, killed her to prove a point, gave her breath again, and said bring me the pearl. We will talk then.

She would not discuss the commissioners until the Pearl is in hand. The appointment is not closed. The Pearl is not in Calveno.

## The red ship in the fog

Because Delmar left La Vasca into the Midchain instead of turning east, Umberlee is collecting in other ways.

The Dead Lady is her collection hull — the Red Lady raised. Dumping cargo is tribute without a priest and without consent. A red ship out of fog. An empty boat left spinning. Holds opened to the sea. No survivors, no negotiation. The drowned crew of the original still work those decks, spectral, silent, recognisable to anyone who sailed with Fisk. For now it is fisherman’s talk out of Kalowe. Nobody on a public dock has tied it to Delmar yet.

She cannot retrieve the Pearl herself. She cannot kill him without losing the captains. So the weather comes off the Maw wrong for the season. Waveservants who were procedural last month know his face before he speaks. Boats vanish on lanes the Uncertainty has already sailed. If he points the bow at the trench, the red hull stands off to the horizon and that clock pauses. If he keeps defying, she stops waiting through staged warnings and takes payment herself.

The tribute system and the Pearl crisis are the same weather seen from two heights. Pay the harbour ledgers and you are buying the procedural peace that may be holding the fissure. Fail them long enough and currents reverse against the tide tables, stone with no known quarry comes up, and missing ships stop being a rumour and start being a rate.

## What the place wants

The Maw does not want anything. The things in it do, and they want incompatible work out of the same eighty feet of workshop.

The Pearl wants souls and a clear signal across the breach. Auralis wants the boundary held, and he is failing. Umberlee wants her instrument back and cannot put a hand on it. The Sentinels want a record that does not make them anyone’s tool. Sunkline wants investor confidence on a rig that has never passed a real trial. Voyvode wants a working deep-shelf engine and is paying for it in names on a plank wall. Tidefall’s sealed orders want a military answer to a trench that is not a military problem. Catarina wants rooms that still have their floors, because the engineering in those rooms would teach a flying machine things no foundry will say aloud. Perrin is being driven, not hired. Delmar’s trapped crews are still in the wreck. Crissdalynn’s map still has a hole the size of the drop-off.

Season One was named for a voyage that never arrived. The party resolved Calveno instead. Season Two is still open Midchain. The Maw is ahead of them, not behind them. Decline is a real choice. The gold rush can die without them. The breach can widen without them. The red ship can keep taking boats in their wake without them. Something larger than the Leviathan can answer a signal nobody has switched off.

Beyond the trench the Outer Reach begins, and no colonial power has a name for what the economy cannot price out there. Stone that does not match any quarry. Currents that run against the tables. A slower pile of unread old records and ships that did not come home. The Maw is the only door that matters. Everything else is staging, salvage, or someone else’s ledger trying to pretend the door is still a line on a chart.
