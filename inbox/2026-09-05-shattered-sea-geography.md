# Capture — Shattered Sea geography (Nick dump 2026-09-05)

Source for wiki-ingest. Do not leave as permanent summary; distill into location hubs.

---

The Shattered Sea is a stretch of water about sixty thousand square miles across, sitting in the open ocean south of Chult, weeks past the last coast anyone in Faerûn still bothers to draw. Two long green arcs wrap a wide central channel. Between them sit reefs, deep cuts, and more islands than anyone has finished naming. The Dravosi Crown holds the western door with inspection papers and guns. Tessarine credit holds the eastern books. Everyone in between pays Umberlee, because the sea does not care whose flag is flying.

Think of the whole thing as a long, bent corridor running west to east.

West is the Galewall: a permanent stacked storm belt, the last weather that still belongs to the charted homelands. Crossing takes about three weeks with a good pilot. One ship in three does not finish. Water goes cold, then black volcanic spires rise out of it. Those are the Ashwalls — last solid ground heading west, first safe lee coming back. Steam vents, sulphur on the wind, giant bats in the warm caves, scorpions in handholds that look useful, vultures after wrecks. Damaged ships gather in the lee and get boarded by people who look like rescue. White-fire crossings are the ones nobody agrees on: rigging fused, spars split outward, stormlight that moved like a bird.

South of the Midchain the Doldrums slide fifty to a hundred miles with the season. Sails go slack. Voices carry a quarter mile clean. A black dragon works the still channels. Westbound captains use those calms as a back door around Strait inspection, if they know which week they are in.

East, both arcs pinch together into the Tail: fewer islands, taller, sharper, cliffs into cold water the color of the trench. Morning fog sits late. Compass starts to lie before you reach the drop. Then the Drowned Maw itself — a trench-floor puncture into the Elemental Plane of Water, not a wall around the ocean but a hole in one place. Antheri works climb the western wall in three tiers. Fisk's five ships went down here when the trench opened under all of them at once. Past the Maw there is no resupply and no landmark a pilot would trust. That water is the Outer Reach. Currents reverse. Stone turns up with no quarry anyone can name. Sailors who have been there do not finish the story.

The inhabited chain between those edges is the Verdant Scatter.

## The northern arc: Crown water

The Crown Islands are the big, broad-shouldered northern row. Forested highlands, deep harbours, pale stone forts built for cannon range. Southern coasts look onto the Central Strait. Northern coasts take open swell. Between the islands the Greyteeth break the surface in long pale ridges and have been ruining charts for three generations. Local pilots charge what they are worth. An adult blue dragon uses those same reefs as cover; captains who fly the right flags and leave an open chest on deck come through routes other people lose to "wrecks." The Crown does not put that in writing.

West to east the row runs like this.

**Calder's Tooth** is the gatekeeper at the Strait's western mouth, not always counted among the five proper Crown Isles but it is the door. Port Tidefall hangs on its west cliff in three tiers: Dockfront at the water, Shelf on the rock, Fort Crestwall on top with the guns. Every hull entering the western Strait is supposed to stop, answer questions, and show papers. Cape Solitude is the headland north of the port. High Line is the feature east of it. An overland track runs south. Tidefall's cold northern water is the Scatter's best fishing ground, worked under the fort's guns.

**Calven** sits about three days' wind east of Tidefall. Lowest of the row: marsh, tidal flats, a farm plateau in the middle. Calveno is the city on the only solid ground, the southeast coast, five canal districts visible from mid-Strait on a clear day. The Dravosi flag flies at the harbour. Tessarine money runs the books. Under Le Paludi, in drains older than both flags, the Rattkin have been living since before either power arrived. Northern and western flats drown walkers who do not know them. Giant crabs work the mud. Hunter sharks work the harbour mouth.

**Harwick** is next, two days east of Calder's Tooth, second-largest island, the army place. The Bight on the southwest holds the largest Ship-of-the-Line anchorage in the Scatter. The Naval Yard has built Crown warships for six generations. The Admiralty sits on the hill above it. Harwick Town lives between the Yard wall and the market wharf. Northwest hills are timber the Yard licenses; older stands they will not cut still have wolves. Eastern marshes give small craft a way around the Yard gates. Nobody from Harwick swims off the wharf.

**Aldenmere** is the quiet one: rolling farms, a stone town, a tidal mere about a mile across in the middle of the island that never quite drains. A narrow inlet ties it to the sea. Giant frogs and toads crowd the reeds. An aging crocodile population holds the inlet. Locals do not swim the mere and do not explain why. The Crown sends a tithe officer twice a year and he leaves again. In a storm gap on the run toward Calveno the crew of the *Uncertainty* thought they saw this island. The gap closed before anyone could be sure.

**Stenmark** is the last Dravosi flag before the Maw. Long and narrow, north–south. Gentle west face for the harbour town and thin farmland. Sheer east face dropping into cold water. On a clear day you can see the colour change where Scatter blue-green goes flat and dark. A Crown signal station on that cliff logs colour, fog, compass, and things the watch will not name. Greyteeth are worst in these approaches. Harpies have nested the east face since before the station. Giant sharks work the shelf. Something under the cliff leaves wakes the fishers have a word for and will not use.

Off the far northeastern edge, **High Eyrie** is a two-hundred-foot basalt stack. The Sentinels have kept a monastery and observation ledgers there for two hundred years and refused three Dravosi deals.

## The road between the arcs

The Central Strait is the shipping road everyone uses. Tidefall owns the western mouth. Nimmik Vollask works the water. Patrol cutters own the official lane. Smart captains leave it.

## The southern arc: the Midchain

South of the Strait the Midchain is a long crowded tropical chain on one shallow shelf, about eighteen hundred miles of inhabited water from Kalowe's approaches to the Tail. Most islands are a half-day sail apart. Water goes turquoise, then a blue so clear the reef shows through. Forest and cliff on the weather side, mangrove and reef flat in the lee. Soundings from last season are a rumour. Deep-draft hulls hate almost every landing except Kalowe. Pilots own the channels. The bands people name — west, north, Teeth, south, east — are a pilot's convenience. No two charts draw them the same.

### Western Midchain

Biggest land, islands stacked into haze, pale green over sand. West of the last island the shelf drops and open sea begins. This is why the free port grew here.

**Kalowe** is three reef-linked islets joined by stone bridges, the whole site ringed by reef with one navigable gap. About five hundred feet across the town, eighteen hundred people who live there, more than double that when the dry dock is full. It takes any paying hull and does not ask about papers. The Chain Council sits in a seized fort on the height because Kalowe will not seat a local ruler. The Waveservant shrine at the gap collects from every hull.

**Penance Bar** is a sandspit shrine off this water. **Ashkevet** is an abandoned Crown penal colony where something still rings the yard bell at dawn. **Sabrique** works indenture and salt pans. Outer islands still see press gangs and a Crown cutter. Channels are wide enough for a frigate and shallow enough that any frigate captain who skips a pilot becomes a wreck.

### Northern Midchain

A dense defended strip two or three islands deep, facing the Strait to the north and the Teeth to the south. Channels so tight a mast shows over the ridge before the hull rounds it. Villages hide in coves and watch both ways. Passage is by consent. A stranger's coin is not placement.

**Murrat** sits between Kalowe and the Teeth: a mile-and-a-half limestone reef that looks empty from the water. Canopy closes twenty feet inland. Dozens of Moucheron kin-villages are built into cliff hollows and tied by rope bridges. Blood is the local currency. Outsiders who land are hunted. The eastern reef is longer than the charts, the tide pulls in, and two wrecks are already on it.

**Sparhold Isle** is a half-mile cut-over islet at the strip's southern lip. Sparhold itself is the timber fortress-market on the harbour slope, closest harbour to the Teeth, cheaper and looser than Kalowe. The last local spar-stand came down when the walls went up; crews now cut the Teeth fringe instead.

**Yssenmoor** is three miles of unclaimed bog under a lid of fog that does not lift. Sound arrives before its source. Sir Quackers holds it. No dry landing.

**The Unplotted** is not an island that forgot to get charted. It is one animal the size of a green hill over a glass bay, waiting where open water should be. It goes where ships will need shelter. Charts disagree because it moves.

Other named ground on this strip, most of it small and particular:

- **Huahei** — marsh, fey, the Glutehorn. You only go there when you already need it.
- **Adwe** — mangrove maze that rearranges after every storm.
- **Corrigan's Rest** — retired pirate crew living comfortably.
- **Barrow Ait** — burial mounds; grave-robbers do not leave with prizes.
- **Drovannis** — bird sanctuary; migrants come back carrying small lost things.
- **Calder's Verge** — glassblowers on a silica vent.
- **Chronehall** — clockmakers timing something they will not name.
- **Old Correspondence** — a scribe still drafting the same unfinished letter.
- **Ilo Maren** — kelp-harvest matriarchy; the annual bonfire lights the bay.
- **Nemwe** — abandoned Dravosi watchtower whose signal-fire still works.
- **Munai** — feral orchard where poisons and cures look alike.
- **Kwoss** — giant sea-turtle nesting; egg season is a fight.
- **Whitmoth Hollow** — albino apes keeping a vanished order's garden.
- **Vetau** — geothermal atoll; midday boil seals the only safe anchorage for an hour.
- **Otanu** — mild volcano; locals read the plume like weather.
- **Thessit** — whole wrecks wash up months after they sank somewhere else.
- **The Foundling Shoals** — unharmed infants appear on the reef.
- **Quillane** — scholar-mages in a slowly sinking tidal observatory.
- **Tor Vessane** — captains route wide; the curse is real and nobody agrees on its shape.
- **Ghal Idrissa** — Crown quarantine. No one sent here leaves.
- **The Long Wager** — one rivalry, generations, one ageless judge.
- **Vaelspire** — basalt said to answer one true question, once.

### The Verdant Teeth

The middle of the Midchain is not packed small islands. It is five country-scale rainforest islands sitting close enough that facing shores are hours, interiors are weeks. Jungle to the tide. Pale reef ring. Beach trade only, and not on every beach. Interiors closed.

**Veth** is the largest, two hundred fifty to three hundred fifty miles on the long axis, first Tooth you cannot mistake from the west. Terra firme: oldest dry-ground rainforest in the chain. One sanctioned western beach twenty strides wide. No harbour. Blue-caste go-betweens on the sand. Higher caste watches from the treeline. Paths that last were left on purpose.

**Sorn** sits on Veth's north face, looking toward the Northern Midchain. Farm heart. Seasonal flood-pulse. The Botukuri work lanes of crop in standing water and lose whatever they cannot recut. Pale sand, the only clean landing on unbroken green, held by a purple garrison. Jean-Claude's sister Simone runs that beach. Raids do not come from here.

**Karath** is the eastern Tooth, loud, blackwater, chemical. No sanctioned landing. Across half a mile of open channel its cliffs face Aruhe's landing beach — close enough to swim. Gold-caste toxin farms hide inland. Midchain coasts report the raids getting worse from this direction.

**Dreth** is lower and wetter. There is no shoreline you can name. Trunks stand in the tide. Canopy joins overhead. The war went under. Axolotls live in the channel silt.

**Orak** is the raised twin: high karst, cloud on the towers, forest only in the clefts, white water falling off the seaward face into Dreth. Linked to Dreth by shallows and one long flooded channel. Forbidden. No trade beach. Expeditions that "do Orak" are usually still in the channel, lying.

### Southern Midchain

Smallest band, hardest to land. Steep volcanic knots, terraces on every slope that will hold them, beaches a few boat-lengths. Deep water to most shores. Landing is a negotiation on the sand before anyone leaves the boat. These islands have never asked the Chain Council for help against Grung pressure.

**Halewick** is an ordinary fishing island walkable in under a day. For three weeks it has been a nine-year-old's kingdom. Toby speaks and the fact stays. Frankie collides with him. Deaf villagers still remember the real island.

**Blackrule** is a Tyr chapterhouse cut into terrace-rock. **Slackwater** is the one shared harbour, tide-calmed by its ruling family.

The rest of the row, each its own small country:

- **Ket Solane** — smugglers' caves with false bottoms the Council has not finished mapping.
- **Feyroa** — a one-still distillery that takes secrets, not coin.
- **Ossira** — seabird rookery so dense the beach shifts under molted feathers.
- **Palutai** — fishing families feuding over one reef; a newcomer picks a side in an hour.
- **Ravasol** — stilt village; rooftop children sell secrets.
- **Sable Roost** — grounded sky-pirate wrecks, each claimed by one survivor.
- **Sanvashti** — Grung neutral ground under a generations-old truce.
- **Sump Cradle** — healing mud that always leaves a lasting odd change.
- **The Hollow Choir** — tide-sung sea caves; any voice comes back as harmony.
- **Twineye** — twin islets, one joining arch, one family still fighting over it.
- **Velk Ithera** — one fungal hillside that ripples when you make a sound.

### Eastern Midchain, Aruhe, and the thin water

East of the Teeth the land thins — except Aruhe.

**Aruhe**, the Hungry Isle, is a mute country: about five hundred miles on the long axis, a hundred fifty at the waist. Charts still treat it as a waypoint. It is not. From Karath the landing is one reef gap, one short shingle beach, old terraces climbing into fruit that hangs too heavy. Behind that the jungle does not stop and does not sing. Grung patrol the western reefs and will not set foot on the sand. A wrecked raiding hull sits above the tideline. Inland the island is bands of the same garden getting sicker: Old Gardens, Quiet, Grasslands where the river forces the trees apart, Rot, a highland Clear Lake, and the Grove on a shelf above one shore. Lava tubes cut under several bands at once. Everything familiar has been pushed past its job. The Bloodhawk owns the open sky over grassland, lake, and the channel. Nothing lives here as a town.

Beyond that mass the lesser eastern islands sit far apart on pale reef you can sail a day without leaving soundings. Oldest water in the chain. Charts agree least. Under the Teikhinos Reef, **Halythion** has never appeared on a colonial survey.

Named landfalls out here:

- **Amberfall** — amber diggings; things frozen mid-motion inside the stone.
- **Cambrey's Fold** — a straight path that returns you to your own footprints.
- **Coeur Brisé** — split arch; a locked chest two families still check.
- **Corvenna** — neutral champion court; a cracked bell ends the dispute.
- **Duskveil** — dusk that never lifts.
- **Ferrymoor** — fog dock, one unaffiliated ferryman, crossing unmapped.
- **Gullmoth Reach** — fey-moth roost; ships come in dark.
- **Lethys** — feral griffon cliffs; a collar that no longer fits.
- **Old Harrow's Bones** — a derelict so large it is an island.
- **Otel Karn** — south of Gullmoth Reach. Death has stopped taking people. Wounds stay. Nothing heals. It looks like paradise until it does not.
- **Ash Caldera** — dead volcanic cone, burned jungle to black glass, crater glow at night. Salvage has gone in. None came back.

Near the Maw edge, **Fathomrush** is the boomtown that stages dives on the Shelfworks — the Antheri salvage field on the western shelf, about forty feet down, then a drop nobody likes to look at. The *Red Lady* wreck sits on the eastern edge of that field. The Tail's unnamed staging island does the same job under another name: last supplies, last hands, last honest gossip before the trench.

## How the pieces sit in the head

West: Galewall, then the Ashwalls.  

Then the two green arcs around the Strait — Crown Isles north, Midchain south — with Calder's Tooth and Tidefall as the western lock.  

In the Midchain's wide middle, five huge Teeth, then Aruhe as a mute nation just east of Karath.  

Then thinning reef, the Tail's cold pinch, the Shelfworks, the Maw, and water the charts refuse.

Most of the Scatter is not empty. It is crowded with places too small for a governor and too particular to ignore. The money is knowing when to leave the official route. The danger is that the unofficial route already belongs to someone who learned it first.
