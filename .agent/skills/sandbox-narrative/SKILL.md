# Sandbox Narrative

Place pressures, toys, and choices in motion; do not write a path the players must follow. A
situation remains alive when it moves without the party and supports multiple valid approaches.

## Four tests

Every major toy needs: **clarity** (actionable without lore dump), **agency** (fight, bargain,
investigate, exploit, redirect, or ignore), **reactivity** (actors move when untouched), and
**portability** (works for different places, parties, and approaches). Add moral, personal, and
opportunistic entry points where appropriate. Replace a required solution with visible pressure,
stakes, clues, and state-based consequences.

## Procedure

1. Inventory current location, fronts/quests, faction resources, deadlines, promises, unresolved
   clues, and off-screen movement from `hot.md` and `qmd-retrieval`.
2. Name who wants what, what happens without intervention, what is visible, and the pressure's
   independent next move.
3. Name at least one PC connection: retrieve two active tensions, a long-term desire, and current
   friction. If no real connection exists, cut the element or ask for one.
4. Offer at least two materially different approaches and a way to walk away.
5. For a hidden conclusion, seed three distinct clues in different nodes/channels; at least two
   must be reachable without combat.
6. Audit away authored PC choices, feelings, success, fixed dialogue, and if/then chains deeper
   than one step. Give opposition pre-existing agency.
7. Write broad outcomes as changed states in relationships, resources, knowledge, position, or
   clocks. Test late arrival, negotiation, avoidance, and an unexpected capability.

Use clocks to make inaction costly, foreshadow conditions rather than one foretold event, and use
in-medias-res openings when they remove dead time. A seed is a loaded possibility, not a promised
payoff. Never mutate canon or runtime in this audit; route actual clock movement to `world-tick`
and post-play truth to `reconciling-session-evidence`.

## Handoffs

`narrative-islands` owns situation topology; `session-beats` owns beat composition; `run-guide`
owns table assembly; `encounter-prep` owns encounter procedure. This skill audits those outputs.
