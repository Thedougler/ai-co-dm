# Calder's Tooth / Port Tidefall
> [!narration] Narration
> Calder's Tooth is a pale stone fort on the western mouth of the Central Strait, and Port Tidefall is the harbour below where every ship queues to show papers. Bells and shouted names carry over salt and tar. The fort sits above the water gate; the inspection lane is the only forward path.


## At a glance
Calder’s Tooth guards the western mouth of the Central Strait; Port Tidefall requires every ship to stop and show papers.
## Five-sentence kernel
- **Function:** A Dravosi gate that turns the Central Strait into inspectable traffic.
- **Fantastic:** A pale stone fort makes paperwork a physical barrier on open water.
- **Conflict:** naval law, commerce, Passage movement, and local survival contest the compulsory stop.
- **Promise:** Cross, bargain, evade, or expose the system while every option changes the record.
- **Trajectory:** a crisis turns inspection into occupation or a public refusal.
## 3Fs and contradiction
- **Fantastic:** fortification reaches into a shipping lane.
- **Familiar:** harbour, gate, papers, queues, clerks, patrols.
- **Functional:** compulsory inspection decides who moves and what becomes known.
- **Contradiction:** a stop meant to secure the route can make the route more dangerous.
- **Signatures:** pale stone; inspection papers; ships waiting under a fort.

## Topology
- `[[Central Strait]] -- approach lane (visible, unavoidable) --> Port Tidefall inspection`.
- `inspection -- paper route (slow, legal, information-rich) --> harbour`.
- `inspection -- water gate (committing, official) --> Central Strait`.
- `harbour -- cliff/shore route (hidden, exposed on land) --> Calder’s Tooth fort` (**seed**).
- **Loop:** approach → inspection → harbour → shore/fort → return lane.
- **Bypass:** Passage/weather/flight/teleport can avoid the stop with honest attention and legal consequences.
- **Retreat:** return to strait before papers are seized or accept detention/negotiation.
- **Vertical connection:** fort above, harbour below, ships between; visibility is leverage.

## Information economy
- **Secret:** inspection can become occupation under crisis.
- **Witness:** clerk/ship captain describes changed practice (**seed**).
- **Trace:** altered stamp, extra berth, moved chain, or seized paper (**seed**).
- **Document:** legal paper conflicts with Tessarine/Passage evidence.
- **Failure cost:** delay, scrutiny, seized access, or new obligation; route remains.
## Pressure
Routine → Alert → Contested → Occupied/Refused: standard stop → increased searches → disputed authority → fort expands or harbour resists.
## Location moves
**Move:** papers change. **Trigger:** raid/front step. **Result:** new questions/stamps. **Opportunity:** compare records or create a legal contradiction. **Consequence:** future entries remember the answer.

**Move:** harbour chain closes. **Trigger:** public alarm. **Result:** ships queue outside. **Opportunity:** land route or negotiate. **Consequence:** Central Strait traffic slows.

**Move:** occupation offered. **Trigger:** local authority declares unable to secure traffic. **Result:** Crown resources arrive. **Opportunity:** organize refusal or use new access. **Consequence:** autonomy shrinks.

## Waveservant Dockfront shrine
At the south end of the Dockfront, the harbour shrine is cut into the cliff with its roof pressed low. Tallow and salt come first. A junior watches the candles while a senior handles the tribute and its record. It is harbour furniture beside the Crown's inspection machinery, not a land temple.
