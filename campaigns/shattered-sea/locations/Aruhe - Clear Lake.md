# Clear Lake

![[attachments/shattered-sea/places/aruhe-the-clear-lake.jpeg|Clear Lake, a wide inland basin cupped by forested walls]]

> Clear Lake fills a cup of steep green walls, wide enough that the far bank thins to a tree line and pale cliff scars. A shelf of black hexagonal stone steps into water so clear that schools of small fish show over round pale rocks and moss. Marsh grass and a slow brown channel gather along one side, then the color falls into deep blue where a long dark body lies under the surface. ## At a glance
Clear Lake is the inland freshwater basin at the uphill end of Aruhe's rivers. A shelf of black hexagonal stone and glass-clear shallows make drinking and camping straightforward. Otter families occupy this water, and a body sixty to eighty feet long uses the deep. The outlet fans downhill into [[Aruhe - River]]; the marsh and stone edge continue toward [[Aruhe - Memorial Grove]]. Stay on a valley rim and the basin can be skipped; stay on the shelf and it remains water and stone.

## Senses
> [!narration] Senses
> Black hexagonal stone shelves into water so clear the bottom still shows, cold and drinkable, with schools of small fish over round pale rocks and moss. Water taps the shelf in small regular hits, and the stone is solid underfoot, slick where moss holds. Marsh grass and a brown channel hold one bank, insects working the reeds, the ground giving into mud. Then the color drops into deep blue, cool wet air coming off the lake, a long dark body lying farther out, and a delayed pressure under the surface more than a splash. The far shore thins to trees and pale scars in the steep green walls.

## Geography
> [!narration] Geography
> The lake sits at the top of Aruhe's water, wide enough that the opposite shore is a thin dark line under steep forest. Black hexagonal stone makes the near camp: drink there, walk it, sleep on it. Just beyond, the shallows stay glass-clear over pale rocks, then the bottom falls away into deep blue. Marsh grass and a slow brown channel gather along one bank, softer going and more cover. The outlet fans downhill. The marsh and the stone edge keep on toward higher green.

Follow [[Aruhe - Grasslands]] uphill along [[Aruhe - River]] to reach the basin. The marsh and shore continue toward [[Aruhe - Memorial Grove]]. Free-flowing seepage from [[Aruhe - Lava Tubes]] can reach this water. A valley rim skips the lake.

## If the party
- **Drink or camp on the shelf:** the water is good and the stone is solid.
- **Wade the shallows:** otters notice. Staying in the water is play.
- **Enter deep water:** otter play, and the long dark body is close.
- **Take fish or anything from the lake:** it belongs to the otter family. See [[Aruhe River Otter]].
- **Follow marsh or shore toward higher green:** [[Aruhe - Memorial Grove]].
- **Follow the outlet downhill:** [[Aruhe - River]].

## Who is here
- **Occupied water:** [[Aruhe River Otter]] families. The lake family is larger than a river family; its matriarch is older than the wreck on the beach. Play, Hunt, and family memory live on the otter note. The lake matriarch is Huge and not statted there.
- **Deep water / adjacent canopy:** [[Great Crown Squid]] (60–80 feet, with tentacles). Combat math lives on the squid note.
- **Margins:** [[Unsaid Macaw]] along orchards and lake edges.
- Wet margins may hold [[Breathmelon]], as on the river. Fish are ecology, not a loot or monster package.

## What is here
### Threats
- Anything entering the water participates in the otters' play. Anything taken from the lake belongs to their family. If a family member is harmed, play stops: ten seconds of silence, then one person is struck from below. Details: [[Aruhe River Otter]].
- The deep holds enormous life at squid scale. Run that fight from [[Great Crown Squid]].
- Marsh mud and reed banks can be Difficult Terrain. The shelf is honest stone. Treat crossing as real distance, not a skip.
### Secrets
- The drinkable water is clean. There is no hidden poison in it.
- The gin-clear look is tended. Otters cull grazers, keep shade off the water, and patrol the banks, the same garden-work as on the river.
