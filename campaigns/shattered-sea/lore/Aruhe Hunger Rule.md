# Aruhe Hunger Rule
> [!narration] Narration
> _Visualizer: Hinewai ingest pending._

## Summary
Aruhe tolerates crossing, watching, sheltering, and passage. The garden minds who takes. Pluck, uproot, trap, fish for yourself, or kill to carry flesh, and surrounding life converges in hostility. Receive what the island offers without claiming a living thing, and that is a different sentence. The response is aligned hunger, not one mind or a master predator.

## Precise distinction
- **Passage tolerated:** Cross the island, watch life, shelter, map, and pass through without claiming a living thing.
- **Taking answered:** Plucking, uprooting, trapping, fishing for self, or killing to carry flesh is a claim. The nearest animals turn.
- **Receiving allowed:** Fruit and other offerings can be genuinely beneficial, with no hidden curse on the flesh; the meaningful distinction is whether someone takes living abundance for ownership or receives what the island offers.
- **Safe nonliving use:** Loose stone and shed shell are acceptable. Free-flowing spring, stream, and cave seepage are drinkable.
- **Living place:** Living things belong where they are. Ownership, waste, and careless destruction offend the island's law.
- **Convergence:** On a claim, surrounding life converges hostilely. The response is ecological alignment, not a single mind or master predator.

## Hinewai's rule
The Hunger Rule is the operational expression of Hinewai's grief. She protected her companion's grave by refusing roots, fungi, insects, and seedlings; that successful preservation metastasized into an island that cannot let life finish. Those who love life are still friends in what remains of her mind. Those who harm life feed life instead.

## Known to
The rule is the operational truth of Aruhe. Surveyors learn it from observation, traces, and consequences. The dump establishes Hinewai as the source of the law; older notes that left the origin unknown are superseded as DM canon, though characters may still lack the autobiography.

## Variants
The name Hungry Isle means too much of everything, not lack. Outsiders may call any disappearance hunger, but the useful distinction is passage versus possession and taking versus receiving.
