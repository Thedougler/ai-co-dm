# Beat-type cards

Select a card because the fiction calls for it, not to fill an empty slot. Every
card names a trigger, stakes, and player options; adjust it to the current state.

## Hook

### Kidnapped
- **Use when:** Someone the players care about is taken, or the players are targets.
- **Do:** Trigger a credible abduction; show who is at risk and time or leverage at stake; offer pursuit, negotiation, investigation, or refusal.
- **Agency note:** Never assume rescue is the goal; let players choose whom to save, whether to bargain, and what cost to accept.

### Coronet Blue (amnesia/role assumed)
- **Use when:** A character wakes without key memory or is mistaken for someone with an urgent role.
- **Do:** Trigger it with evidence and immediate stakes; show the assumed duty and conflicting clues; offer playing along, exposing the mistake, investigating, or leaving.
- **Agency note:** Do not dictate identity, memories, or allegiance; let the player define what the gap and role mean.

### Play a Cliffhanger as Hook
- **Use when:** The session should open in immediate peril, pursuit, attack, or an obstacle.
- **Do:** Trigger action with a legible objective; show what can be lost and an off-ramp; offer assist, hide, flee, bargain, or exploit the danger.
- **Agency note:** Urgency must focus choices, not remove them; do not pre-resolve the opening action.

### Play a Development as Hook
- **Use when:** A clue, offer, conversation, or relationship change is the strongest opening.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a meaningful question or opportunity; show why it matters now and what is at stake; offer follow, refuse, investigate, negotiate, or redirect.
- **Agency note:** Let players set the priority and interpretation; do not force the prepared mystery or job.

### Discovery
- **Use when:** A place, object, witness, pattern, or fact can open a new direction.
- **Do:** Trigger it through observable fiction; show immediate stakes and at least two leads or uses; offer study, share, exploit, hide, or ignore it.
- **Agency note:** Reveal what is findable, not what players must conclude; let them choose which implication matters.

### Crisis
- **Use when:** A visible danger, deadline, or breakdown demands a response now.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a concrete change; show who or what suffers and the deadline; offer intervention, triage, delegation, escape, or acceptance.
- **Agency note:** A crisis is pressure, not a command; consequences follow choices, including choosing not to help.

### Revelation
- **Use when:** A hidden truth can reframe the opening problem without ending it.
- **Do:** Trigger it with evidence or a credible witness; show the stakes of knowing and at least two responses; offer confront, conceal, verify, ally, or exploit.
- **Agency note:** Do not dictate the players' reaction or make the revelation solve everything automatically.

### Murder
- **Use when:** A death creates an urgent question, danger, or power vacuum.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a discoverable death; show victims, suspects, stakes, and time pressure; offer investigate, protect, accuse, bargain, or walk away.
- **Agency note:** Preserve multiple theories and outcomes; do not require solving the case before other goals can matter.

### False Accusation
- **Use when:** Players or an ally are blamed for a crime or harm they may not have caused.
- **Do:** Trigger it with public evidence or a powerful accuser; show the cost of conviction and response routes; offer defend, flee, expose, negotiate, or leverage the claim.
- **Agency note:** Let players accept, contest, redirect, or exploit the accusation; do not predetermine guilt or vindication.

### Looming Threat
- **Use when:** A danger is approaching but has not yet struck.
- **Do:** Trigger it with signs, a clock, or a credible warning; show what happens if it advances and how players can affect it; offer prepare, investigate, recruit, evacuate, confront, or ignore.
- **Agency note:** Make the threat legible and alterable; its arrival and form respond to player choices.

## Cliffhanger

### Chase
- **Use when:** A target, pursuer, escape route, or moving objective matters immediately.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a clear lead; show terrain, stakes, and what losing ground changes; offer speed, shortcuts, obstacles, ambush, surrender, or abandonment.
- **Agency note:** Let players change the route or objective; do not make capture or escape automatic.

### Pursuit
- **Use when:** An enemy or authority is closing in across scenes or locations.
- **Do:** Trigger it when the pursuer gains a credible advantage; show the clock, evidence, and stakes; offer hide, misdirect, negotiate, turn the tables, or flee.
- **Agency note:** Track visible progress and counterplay; do not teleport pursuers into position because the chart says so.

### Race
- **Use when:** Actors compete to reach, secure, or complete something first.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a shared deadline and finish condition; show routes, costs, and stakes; offer speed, sabotage, cooperation, shortcuts, or changing the goal.
- **Agency note:** Resolve choices and agreed procedures transparently; let players lose, tie, or redefine success.

### Fist Fight
- **Use when:** A personal dispute or close-quarters clash is the immediate pressure.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a credible flashpoint; show objectives, stakes, surroundings, and exit costs; offer fight, grapple, flee, de-escalate, or use the environment.
- **Agency note:** Do not force combat from an insult; honor surrender, retreat, negotiation, and nonlethal aims.

### Dogfight
- **Use when:** Flyers, pilots, or vehicles contest position in dangerous space.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a moving objective; show terrain, ranges, stakes, and failure consequences; offer attack, evade, escort, board, flee, or exploit the environment.
- **Agency note:** Make movement and objectives matter more than attrition; let pilots disengage or pursue another win condition.

### Confrontation (verbal pressure)
- **Use when:** A tense exchange can force a decision without physical combat.
- **Do:** Trigger it when interests collide; show each side's leverage, stakes, and claims; offer persuade, threaten, bargain, expose, lie, leave, or concede.
- **Agency note:** Treat words as consequential actions, not a cutscene; do not decide what a player says or what an NPC must accept.

### Duel
- **Use when:** One-on-one skill, honor, proof, or personal stakes focus the danger.
- **Do:** Trigger it by a specific challenge or agreed objective; show rules, stakes, terrain, and alternatives; offer accept, refuse, cheat, interrupt, negotiate, or withdraw.
- **Agency note:** Consent and stakes must be meaningful; a duel is not the only path to resolve conflict.

### Battle
- **Use when:** Multiple combatants and a consequential objective make a larger fight fictionally necessary.
- **Do:** Trigger it with opposing plans; show objectives, terrain, stakes, and off-ramps; optionally dial opposition: **Grunts** (3:1 weaker) → **Minions** (2:1 slightly weaker) → **Henchmen** (equal/slightly stronger) → **Villain** (substantially stronger + henchmen).
- **Agency note:** The dial is optional, not a mandate; let players choose objectives, alliances, retreat, surrender, or another solution, without stacked fights absent recovery or changed stakes.

### Monster
- **Use when:** A creature's presence creates immediate fear, danger, or a survival problem.
- **Do:** Trigger it with signs and a credible encounter; show what it wants, what is at stake, and escape or study options; use a visible **Scare → Fright → Horror → Terror** dial as fiction and choices warrant.
- **Agency note:** Escalate fear through information and consequences, not helpless surprise; let players hide, flee, parley, exploit weaknesses, or confront it.

### Ambush
- **Use when:** An actor has a credible position and strikes before players are ready.
- **Do:** Trigger it with prior motive and opportunity; show the immediate threat, stakes, and cover or routes; offer fight, flee, surrender, counterambush, bargain, or protect an objective.
- **Agency note:** Telegraph enough for counterplay; do not use surprise to erase decisions or guarantee damage.

### Obstacles/Traps
- **Use when:** A barrier or hazard tests attention, tools, movement, or planning.
- **Do:** Trigger it through discoverable signs; provide prior or current clues, stakes, worsening effects, and multiple solutions, bypasses, or costly alternatives.
- **Agency note:** A trap cannot be a blind guess or single hidden answer; failures change the situation rather than stopping play.

### Contest
- **Use when:** Opposed actors race, persuade, track, hide, or perform under comparable stakes.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a shared objective; state stakes, procedure, information, and what each result changes; offer tactics, preparation, risk, cooperation, or withdrawal.
- **Agency note:** No GM fiat: use transparent skill, choices, and agreed rolls, including meaningful partial outcomes.

### Skirmish
- **Use when:** A short clash can change position, access, resources, or information without being the main battle.
- **Do:** Trigger it around a concrete objective; show stakes, terrain, and exit conditions; offer fight, distract, steal, rescue, retreat, negotiate, or bypass.
- **Agency note:** Keep it brief and consequential; do not add a skirmish as filler or require total enemy defeat.

## Development

### Warning
- **Use when:** Evidence of impending danger can change a decision before impact.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a credible sign or messenger; show what is at stake and what preparation can alter; offer heed, verify, prepare, warn others, or ignore.
- **Agency note:** Make warnings actionable, not compulsory; ignoring one produces grounded consequences.

### Hidden Weakness
- **Use when:** A threat has a discoverable limitation that opens alternatives to brute force.
- **Do:** Trigger it through observation, experiment, or a source; show the stakes of learning it and ways to exploit it; offer test, share, prepare, bypass, or attack.
- **Agency note:** Do not hide the only answer behind one roll; let players find, invent, or reject approaches with consequences.

### Revelation
- **Use when:** New truth changes the meaning of an active situation.
- **Do:** Trigger it with evidence; show who benefits, what is at stake, and at least two decisions it enables; offer verify, confront, conceal, ally, or exploit.
- **Agency note:** Reveal information, not a prescribed response; keep uncertainty where evidence warrants it.

### Advantage Revealed
- **Use when:** Players learn they possess a resource, position, ally, or leverage they can use.
- **Do:** Trigger it when evidence makes the advantage credible; show its stakes, limits, and costs; offer spend, save, share, trade, or keep it hidden.
- **Agency note:** Do not grant an automatic win; players decide whether and how to turn it into change.

### Clue
- **Use when:** Information can open a route, identify an actor, or alter priorities.
- **Do:** Trigger it through an observable source; show why it matters and what is at stake; offer follow, test, combine, publish, sell, or ignore it.
- **Agency note:** Provide redundant or varied paths to important truths; a missed clue changes options but does not end play.

### Retreat
- **Use when:** Withdrawal can preserve lives, resources, or a better position.
- **Do:** Trigger it when pressure makes staying costly; show what is lost and what can be saved; offer withdraw, delay, bargain, cover an ally, or risk staying.
- **Agency note:** Treat retreat as a real success condition with consequences, not cowardice or a forced reset.

### Hesitation
- **Use when:** A person or faction pauses because competing motives make action uncertain.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a credible conflict; show stakes and what could tip the choice; offer persuade, exploit, reassure, pressure, wait, or act around them.
- **Agency note:** Do not freeze an NPC to preserve a plot; response follows player pressure and established interests.

### Mistaken Identity
- **Use when:** Someone is plausibly treated as another person, faction, or role.
- **Do:** Trigger it with evidence and social cost; show who gains or suffers and how the error can be used or corrected; offer play along, reveal, investigate, flee, or redirect.
- **Agency note:** Do not dictate a player's identity or consent; let players choose whether to exploit or end the mistake.

### Villain Monologue
- **Use when:** An antagonist has reason to explain terms, motives, or a threat in the players' presence.
- **Do:** Trigger it through leverage or a pause in action; show what information and stakes are exposed; offer listen, interrupt, challenge, bargain, attack, or use the time.
- **Agency note:** Keep it interruptible and fallible; speech creates openings, not immunity or forced agreement.

### Secret Meeting
- **Use when:** An unseen negotiation or alliance can change faction intent.
- **Do:** Trigger it when actors have motive and privacy; show stakes and clues players can discover; offer attend, spy, infiltrate, expose, interrupt, or let it happen.
- **Agency note:** A secret is not a cutscene; make detection and intervention possible and let terms respond to play.

### Personal Stake
- **Use when:** A conflict touches a character's bond, vow, identity, or cost.
- **Do:** Trigger it with established history; show what can be gained or lost and meaningful approaches; offer protect, confront, sacrifice, refuse, recruit, or redefine the goal.
- **Agency note:** Present pressure without assigning a player's feelings or choice; the player authors the response.

### Second Chance
- **Use when:** A rare opportunity could avert a failure at a meaningful price.
- **Do:** Trigger it through established resources or a credible offer; show the stakes and attached cost or danger; offer accept, refuse, negotiate, or seek a harder alternative.
- **Agency note:** Make it rare, costly, and player-facing; never erase failure or its consequences for convenience.

### Gain Mastery
- **Use when:** Practice, insight, or sacrifice earns a new capability or reliable edge.
- **Do:** Trigger it through demonstrated learning; show limits, stakes, and uses; offer apply now, train further, teach, trade, or reserve it.
- **Agency note:** Let mastery emerge from play and choices; it opens options, not guaranteed outcomes.

### Alliance
- **Use when:** Actors with compatible interests can cooperate against a shared problem.
- **Do:** Trigger it with mutual leverage; state terms, stakes, trust limits, and exit conditions; offer ally, demand proof, set terms, use temporary cooperation, or decline.
- **Agency note:** Keep allies autonomous and interests active; players may renegotiate or abandon the alliance.

### Betrayal
- **Use when:** An ally's conflicting interest can turn trust into danger.
- **Do:** Trigger it with motive and an observable tell; show stakes and chances to test, prevent, expose, or exploit it; offer confront, watch, bargain, turn the tables, or accept risk.
- **Agency note:** Betrayal must be detectable and consequential, not a secret GM switch; surprise cannot deny fair clues.

### Sabotage
- **Use when:** Someone damages a plan, resource, route, or tool from within or nearby.
- **Do:** Trigger it through motive and access; show signs, stakes, and detection or repair options; offer inspect, secure, repair, expose, counter-sabotage, or proceed damaged.
- **Agency note:** Give a fair chance to notice or prevent it; do not reveal sabotage only to force the next prepared scene.

### Foreshadowing
- **Use when:** A future danger, opportunity, or relationship deserves meaningful early signs.
- **Do:** Trigger it with concrete recurring evidence; show why noticing matters and what is at stake; offer investigate, prepare, warn, exploit, or dismiss it.
- **Agency note:** Foreshadowing is not a promise that the forecast must occur; players can prevent, redirect, or reinterpret it.

### Not What It Seems
- **Use when:** A credible surface explanation hides a more useful or dangerous truth.
- **Do:** Trigger it with contradictions; show the stakes of being wrong and evidence for alternatives; offer test, trust, expose, use the appearance, or walk away.
- **Agency note:** Play fair with clues and interpretations; do not invalidate conclusions without new fiction.

### Strange Bedfellows
- **Use when:** Rivals must cooperate because a threat or opportunity outweighs their conflict.
- **Do:** Trigger it with shared pressure; show terms, stakes, distrust, and exit routes; offer cooperate, set conditions, exploit, betray, or refuse.
- **Agency note:** Preserve each side's agency and interests; cooperation need not become friendship or a mandatory alliance.

### Turnabout
- **Use when:** A consequence or new fact reverses who has leverage or what success means.
- **Do:** Trigger it with an earned reversal; show new stakes and options; offer press the advantage, recover, negotiate, flee, or change objectives.
- **Agency note:** Base reversals on visible choices and fiction, not arbitrary GM correction; players may keep or lose the advantage.

### Romance
- **Use when:** Attraction, intimacy, or commitment can change choices and relationships.
- **Do:** Trigger it through mutual, established interest; show emotional and practical stakes; offer pursue, pause, define terms, refuse, keep private, or redirect.
- **Agency note:** Keep consent explicit and player-controlled; never force a character's feelings or compliance.

### Lie Revealed
- **Use when:** A false claim, cover story, or omission becomes actionable.
- **Do:** Trigger it with evidence or contradiction; show who is endangered and what choices remain; offer confess, confront, verify, leverage, forgive, or walk away.
- **Agency note:** Let players decide how to respond and liars have motives and options; revelation need not dictate punishment.

### Hazardous Quest
- **Use when:** Reaching a place or securing an object requires sustained risk rather than one obstacle.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a concrete objective; show route, hazards, stakes, resources, and alternatives; offer prepare, choose a route, recruit, turn back, or accept a cost.
- **Agency note:** Make the route a decision space with clues and trade-offs, not a fixed sequence of hazards.

### Puzzle
- **Use when:** Reasoning or experimentation can unlock progress, knowledge, or a safer route.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a meaningful mechanism; provide sufficient clues, stakes, and multiple solution paths or bypasses; offer test, combine, ask, brute-force at a cost, or leave it.
- **Agency note:** Never require one unhinted answer; failed guesses change time, resources, or danger instead of halting play.

### Framed
- **Use when:** Evidence falsely links a character or faction to wrongdoing.
- **Do:** Trigger it with a plausible accusation; show legal, social, or physical stakes and evidence to challenge; offer defend, investigate, flee, bargain, expose, or exploit.
- **Agency note:** Let players choose reputation versus other goals; exoneration is not the only meaningful route.

### Obsession
- **Use when:** A person or faction pursues one aim despite mounting costs.
- **Do:** Trigger it with established motive and escalation; show what the obsession threatens and leverage points; offer appeal, exploit, interrupt, support, or let consequences land.
- **Agency note:** Do not commandeer a player's mind; NPC obsession is pressure, not guaranteed behavior from PCs.

### Back from the Dead
- **Use when:** Established magic, technology, or fiction makes return from death possible and relevant.
- **Do:** Trigger it through a credible mechanism; show stakes, costs, changes, and alternatives; offer accept, refuse, investigate, bargain, or let the death stand.
- **Agency note:** Keep it rare and costly; never restore someone unharmed just to erase sacrifice or failure.

### Rescuers
- **Use when:** Allies or strangers can intervene after players create or encounter danger.
- **Do:** Trigger help through prior relationships, signals, or credible opportunity; show what rescuers can save and their stakes; offer call, direct, trust, bargain, or proceed alone.
- **Agency note:** Rescuers add options, not automatic salvation; players choose whether to accept help and consequences remain.

### Vengeance
- **Use when:** Harm or loss motivates a character or faction to pursue retribution.
- **Do:** Trigger it with an established grievance; show targets, stakes, evidence, and alternatives; offer confront, mediate, aid, redirect, forgive, or refuse.
- **Agency note:** Do not assume revenge is righteous or required; affected players and NPCs choose their response.

## Climax

### Final Revelation
- **Use when:** The highest-stakes confrontation is deciding, exposing, or surviving a truth rather than trading blows.
- **Do:** Trigger it after choices make the truth unavoidable; show who gains, what is at stake, and options to expose, conceal, bargain, sacrifice, or act.
- **Agency note:** The revelation must be earned by prior play and can lead to multiple outcomes; do not force a battle afterward.

### Final Battle
- **Use when:** Player choices have earned a last high-stakes fight as the central confrontation.
- **Do:** Trigger it with established opposing goals; show objective, stakes, terrain, and alternatives; offer fight, negotiate, retreat, protect, sabotage, surrender, or change the win condition.
- **Agency note:** A climax can be any other high-stakes confrontation players earned; do not require a battle, villain, or total victory.

## Resolution

### Happy Ending
- **Use when:** Player choices produce safety, reunion, justice, freedom, or another earned good outcome.
- **Do:** Trigger it after the central question settles; show who benefits, what it costs, and remaining options; offer celebration, repair, redistribution, or a new commitment.
- **Agency note:** Make the joy follow fiction and choices; do not erase losses or dictate what happiness means to players; any sequel follows the changed fiction and player interest.

### Villain Killed
- **Use when:** The antagonist dies as a consequence of the climax and the death changes the world.
- **Do:** Trigger it through player action or established fallout; show power vacuums, costs, and choices; offer claim, flee, reconcile, investigate, or rebuild.
- **Agency note:** Do not require the kill or preserve a sequel; any sequel follows the changed fiction and player interest.

### Villain Surrenders
- **Use when:** The antagonist yields because players' leverage, terms, or actions make resistance untenable.
- **Do:** Trigger it with credible loss or negotiation; show terms, risks, and alternatives; offer arrest, mercy, exchange, trial, execution, or release.
- **Agency note:** Let players decide terms and trust; a sequel follows only if surrender's consequences in the fiction leave a live thread.

### Villain Escapes
- **Use when:** The antagonist gets away through established opportunity, player choice, or credible failure.
- **Do:** Trigger it with visible means and costs; show what is lost, learned, and still possible; offer pursuit, regroup, expose, bargain, or end the pursuit.
- **Agency note:** Escape is not a scripted rematch; any sequel follows fiction and player interest, not GM preservation.

### Heroes Captured
- **Use when:** Players lose access or freedom and capture is a real consequence, not a cutscene.
- **Do:** Trigger it through fair opposition or player choice; show restraints, stakes, knowledge, and escape or negotiation options; offer resist, bargain, deceive, wait, or accept terms.
- **Agency note:** Preserve meaningful choices while captured; a sequel follows the resulting fiction, not a forced breakout.

### Heroes Escape
- **Use when:** Players win freedom from captivity, pursuit, or a collapsing situation.
- **Do:** Trigger it through player planning, risk, or earned opportunity; show what is saved, lost, and now at stake; offer flee, rescue others, hide, strike back, or settle.
- **Agency note:** Escape does not erase capture's costs; any sequel follows changed fiction and player goals.

### Ending Cliffhanger
- **Use when:** Unresolved danger or opportunity is a meaningful endpoint rather than an unfinished scene.
- **Do:** Trigger it after tonight's central question has enough closure; show the concrete new threat, stakes, and player-facing options; offer pursue, prepare, refuse, or end here.
- **Agency note:** A sequel follows the fiction and player interest; do not withhold resolution merely to demand another session.

### Greater Threat
- **Use when:** The climax reveals a larger danger that genuinely follows from what happened.
- **Do:** Trigger it through established evidence or consequences; show why it matters and what players can choose next; offer investigate, prepare, ally, prevent, or walk away.
- **Agency note:** The greater threat is an option, not a mandate; a sequel follows fiction, and current resolution honors player choices.
